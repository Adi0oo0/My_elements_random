About

Equations first, interfaces second.

I'm Aditya, a Computer Science & Engineering student specializing in AI/ML. Most of what I build starts the same way — with a question that has a precise, checkable answer: does this curve plot correctly, does this model classify correctly, does this prompt actually get a better output.

That instinct is why my projects look a little scattered on the surface — a maths visualizer, a prompt generator, a digit classifier, an e-commerce site for ceramics — but underneath, they're the same habit applied to different materials: take something fuzzy, give it structure, and make it usable by someone who isn't me.

I'm still early in the AI/ML side of things — comfortable with Python, getting hands-on with PyTorch — and I'd rather say that plainly than dress it up. The digit classifier at 98% accuracy is the proof I point to when I want to show, not tell, where that's headed.

How I work

Three habits, repeated across every project.

Understand the math

Before writing code, I work out what's actually happening — the equation, the gradient, the transformation.

Ship something real

A working, clickable version beats a perfect plan. I'd rather have a rough demo than a polished idea.

Make it legible

Whether it's a plotted curve or a product grid, the interface should explain itself without a manual.
